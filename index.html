How it works

A simple rhythm.

Short, timed stretches of focus with deliberate rest in between. Each phase has its own colour, so a glance at the screen tells you whether to keep going or step away.

  1. 01

    Focus 25m

    Pick one task. Give it your full attention until the ring closes.

  2. 02

    Short break 5m

    Stand up, stretch, get some water. Away from the screen is best.

  3. 03

    Long break 15m

    After four pomodoros, rest properly before the next cycle begins.

Why it works

Simple on purpose.

A tomato-shaped kitchen timer started it in the late 1980s. The ideas behind it have held up since.

Attention needs rest

Focus runs down the longer you hold it. Regular breaks let it recover before it runs out, so your last session of the day can look like your first.

Attention restoration

A deadline you can see

Work expands to fill the time it's given. A visible twenty-five-minute limit keeps tasks a sensible size and makes starting feel smaller.

Parkinson's law and timeboxing

Energy comes in waves

Alertness rises and falls through the day. A steady cycle of focus and rest works with those swings instead of pushing through them.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I need an account?

Yes, a free one. Sign in with Google, or sign up with your email address. Your sessions are saved to your account, so your count and history follow you to any device.

What counts toward the 8 free sessions?

Every finished phase: focus sessions and breaks both count. When you reach eight in a day, the count starts over at midnight. Premium has no limit.

Can I change the durations?

The free plan uses the classic rhythm: 25 minutes of focus, a 5-minute break, and 15 minutes after every fourth session. With Premium you can set each phase from 1 to 120 minutes, move the long break to every 2 to 8 sessions, or pick a preset.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. Every screen is designed for phones as well as desktops, so you can open thepomodoro.net in your phone's browser and sign in. An installable version (a PWA) is coming soon: add it to your home screen and it opens like any other app, full screen and one tap away.

Why “pomodoro”?

It's Italian for tomato. Francesco Cirillo came up with the technique as a student in the late 1980s, timing his study sessions with a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. That's why the logo is a tomato too.
